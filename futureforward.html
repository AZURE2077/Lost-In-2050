// Present Day //

AI Changing The World — Now

AI isn't a future concept. It's already reshaping healthcare, education, transport, and more — right now, today. Here's what's happening.

🏥
Healthcare
AI diagnostic tools can detect cancer, eye disease, and cardiac conditions earlier and more accurately than human doctors — saving lives at scale.
↳ Google DeepMind's AlphaFold solved protein folding, revolutionizing drug discovery
🎓
Education
AI tutors adapt in real time to each student's learning pace. Tools like Khan Academy's Khanmigo give every student a personal tutor, for free.
↳ AI is reducing dropout rates by identifying struggling students early
🚗
Transport
Self-driving vehicles are operating in real cities. AI optimizes traffic flow, cutting commute times and emissions across urban networks.
↳ Waymo completes 50,000+ driverless rides per week in the US
🌆
Smart Cities
Cities use AI to manage energy grids, predict infrastructure failure, and optimize emergency response — making urban living safer and greener.
↳ Singapore's AI grid reduces city energy usage by up to 20%
💼
The Workplace
AI copilots are augmenting nearly every profession — from lawyers to coders to radiologists. Productivity is soaring, but roles are shifting fast.
↳ GitHub Copilot writes 46% of code in projects that use it
🎨
Creative Industries
AI generates music, film scripts, visual art, and advertising — forcing entire industries to rethink what human creativity means and is worth.
↳ AI music tools are used in 30%+ of commercial ad production

// The Boy Out Of Time //

Meet Savvy Sven

A 16-year-old from Stockholm who accidentally time-travelled to 2050 — and became our guide to the AI-powered world we're all heading toward.

SAVVY SVEN
STOCKHOLM, 2026
⏱ 2026 → 2050
His Story

Sven Karlsson is sixteen years old, perpetually wide-eyed, and almost always the least technologically capable person in any room — which, in the year 2050, is saying something.

Born in Stockholm in 2010, Sven stumbled into time travel the way most teenagers stumble into trouble: by touching something he absolutely should not have touched. A prototype temporal displacement device, buried in his uncle's cluttered garage, sent him lurching twenty-four years into the future with nothing but a half-charged phone, a hoodie, and an alarming lack of preparation.

The 2050 he landed in is barely recognisable. AI doesn't just assist life here — it is life. Cities think. Buildings breathe. Doctors retired a decade ago. Schools dissolved into personalised intelligence ecosystems. Even grief counselling and government policy run through AI frameworks that have quietly made human inefficiency feel quaint.

Why He's Our Guide

What makes him Savvy Sven — a nickname the locals gave him with gentle sarcasm — is his uncanny ability to navigate an overwhelming world through sheer social intelligence. Where others rely on AI to read a room, Sven reads people. He notices hesitation behind a smile, tension in a handshake, the slight pause before someone says they're fine.

In a world that has largely optimised emotion away, his raw human intuition feels almost like a superpower. He carries a battered notebook. He asks questions out loud. He gets lost constantly. But somehow, in a world that has answers for everything, Savvy Sven keeps asking the ones nobody thought to programme.
